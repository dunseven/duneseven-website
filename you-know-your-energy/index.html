The energy-based planner with no wearable required.

“Nobody needs a sensor to tell them they’re exhausted. You knew before your watch did.”

There is a new promise being made in planning apps: we’ll read your energy and plan your day for you.

Here is how that promise works. You buy a wearable. You give an app your heart rate, your sleep data, your movement patterns. An algorithm decides how much energy you have. Then it rearranges your day accordingly — whether you agree with it or not.

We think that promise gets one thing right and everything else wrong.

The part they got right

Energy-first planning is correct. Your capacity is not the same at 7am as it is at 3pm. It is not the same on the day after a night shift as it is after nine hours of sleep. Any planner that treats every hour as identical is lying to you, and you feel that lie as guilt — the gap between what the plan demanded and what your body could give.

We built Dune Seven Planner on that truth in 2019, back when it was a paper planner and “energy-based planning” wasn’t a category yet. Plan around energy, not guilt. It has been our first principle from the first page.

The part they got wrong

You do not need a sensor to know how you feel.

You already know. You knew the moment you woke up. If you live with ADHD, you know something the sensors don’t: your energy doesn’t always follow your sleep score. You can sleep nine hours and wake up foggy. You can sleep five and hit a hyperfocus streak that moves mountains. A wearable reading your heart rate cannot see that. You can.

So we do something radical. We ask you.

You choose your energy. The plan bends around it. That’s the entire mechanism — and it changes everything about whose day it is.

What this means in black and white

No extra equipment. Your planner should not require a $400 device on your wrist before it works properly. Dune Seven works with what you already own: your phone and your self-knowledge.

No biometric data leaving your phone. We never see your heart rate, your sleep, or your movement — because we never ask for it. There is nothing to harvest, nothing to leak, and nothing to sell.

No algorithm overriding you. Whether it reads a sensor on your wrist or watches your calendar quietly for patterns, the moment an app decides what your energy is, it has quietly decided it knows you better than you do. We refuse that premise completely. An app that tells you your energy is just guilt with sensors.

Your self-knowledge, taken seriously. Choosing your own energy level is a thirty-second act of honesty each day. Over weeks, it becomes something bigger: proof that you can read yourself, trust yourself, and plan like it.

The self-fulfilling prophecy problem

There is a name for what happens when a device tells you how your day will go: a self-fulfilling prophecy.

Your watch says your readiness is 42%. So you brace for a bad day. You lower your effort before you’ve tested it. You move carefully, expect less, achieve less — and by evening, the prediction has come true. Not because the sensor was right, but because you believed it before you believed yourself.

The truth of it

The number didn’t describe your day. It wrote it.

Do not be led astray by data collected on your wrist. You are a human — global and holistic, not a heart rate and a sleep score. Your energy is shaped by things no sensor will ever see: what’s on your mind, who you love, what you’re carrying, what you’re hoping for. No one knows you but you.

Who this is for

This is for the shift worker whose “morning” starts at 6pm. The parent running on broken sleep. The ADHD brain whose energy arrives in waves no sensor can chart. The person who is tired of being told by a device how they feel.

You are not a data stream. You are the only person who has ever actually been inside your body. Your planner should act like it.

A human-driven planner, on purpose

Frequent AI users report burnout more than people who barely use it at all. We don’t think that’s a coincidence — it’s what happens when a tool starts making decisions that were always meant to be yours.

Dune Seven uses AI in exactly one place: when a task feels too big to start, I’m Stuck breaks it into smaller steps, and nothing else. It doesn’t read your calendar, doesn’t schedule your day, and doesn’t quietly learn your patterns to decide things for you. That boundary isn’t a limitation waiting to be lifted — it’s the whole point.

The bottom line

Energy-based planning is the future — the whole category agrees now. The only question left is who decides what your energy is: an algorithm reading your wrist, or you.

We made our choice in 2019. You know your energy. We just plan around it.
