Postpartum, with a brain that isn't quite yours yet

Broken sleep, fog, and a mental load that doubled overnight. Dune Seven was built by a mother in exactly this stage, for exactly this stage.

Your brain and body aren't at full capacity right now, and that's valid. Plan for where you actually are, not where you used to be.

Postpartum planner

The fog is real, and it isn't a character flaw

Postpartum cognitive change is well documented: fragmented sleep, hormonal shift and relentless vigilance affect working memory, word-finding and the ability to hold a sequence of steps in your head. You put the phone in the fridge. You lose the thread mid-sentence. You forget the thing you were told four minutes ago.

Then you open a planner still calibrated to who you were eighteen months ago, and it quietly tells you you've gone backwards.

What changes when the app expects less of you

Low mode, for most days at first

Switch to Low and the home screen contracts to something you can actually meet on four hours of broken sleep. Nothing is deleted — every task stays reachable — but you're not asked to face all of it at once. On the days the fog lifts, Flow is right there.

Capture, because you will not remember

The health nurse appointment, the thing your mother said, the nappy size you've outgrown. Two taps and it's out of a working memory that currently has no room. This is the single feature postpartum users use most.

Three priorities, and a shower counts

What matters most today caps at three. In the fourth trimester, "eat something" and "get outside" are entirely legitimate entries. There's no category police and no productivity score.

Habits, with the guilt removed

Water, movement, nutrition and read. Track them if it helps, ignore them if it doesn't. No streak punishment, no cap, no broken chain to feel bad about during a growth spurt.

Dune Seven was built by Angela Kocek out of her own postpartum experience — it's the planner she needed and couldn't find.

When the mental load doubles

A new baby doesn't add tasks so much as it adds continuous background monitoring: feeds, wake windows, appointments, milestones, and the household that still exists underneath all of it. If you were already the default rememberer, you now hold two full indexes at once. That's mental load, and it's the thing most postpartum exhaustion is actually made of.

If it's more than tiredness

Postpartum depression and anxiety are common and treatable, and they're not the same as fog or exhaustion. If you're struggling with persistent low mood, intrusive thoughts or anxiety, please speak to your GP, maternal health nurse, or contact PANDA (1300 726 306) in Australia. A planner sits alongside that support, never in place of it.

Questions

Postpartum planner questions

Is this a baby tracker?

No. Dune Seven doesn't track feeds, sleep or nappies. It's a planner for you — your day, your priorities, your mental load. Plenty of people run a baby tracker alongside it.

I can barely use my phone one-handed. Is it simple enough?

That was a real design constraint. Capture takes two taps, priorities cap at three, and Low mode strips the home screen down to the minimum. There's nothing to configure before you can use it.

Is baby brain actually real?

Postpartum cognitive change is well documented and driven by fragmented sleep, hormonal shift and sustained vigilance. It affects working memory and word-finding. It's real, it's common, and it isn't a permanent state.
