You wrote the list.
Then forgot the list.

You checked your watch three times and saw the time on the fourth. You started twelve things before lunch. None of that is a character flaw. Dune Seven is the ADHD planner built around the brain you actually have: Flow, Low or Focus, chosen by you each morning. Three priorities, not thirty. I’m Stuck for the task that will not start.

Some days you have everything. Some days you have the basics.

Tap Flow and your three priorities, your habits and Today at a glance are all in front of you. Tap Low and Home quiets to the one task you can carry, with everything else held for another time. Tap Focus and the world narrows to one thing. Nothing is deleted when you switch. There is just less asking something of you.

Most planners rest on three quiet assumptions: steady energy, a reliable working memory and an accurate sense of how long a thing takes. ADHD interrupts all three. So the planner keeps working perfectly and you keep feeling like the broken part.

Dune Seven starts from the day you actually have. Every task stays reachable in all three modes. What changes is how much you have to face at once, which is usually the real barrier.

Twenty minutes staring at the same line.

“Book the appointment” is not one task. It is find the number, check the calendar, decide on a day, make the call, and each one has its own wall. Tap I’m Stuck and the task becomes three to five steps you can actually start, with the first step handed to you.

Read on

Task initiation is the wall, not the task. I’m Stuck never judges the task or the delay. It breaks the task down and hands you the first step. Three a month on Free, unlimited on Pro.

If you do not see the thought, it is not there.

The thing you remembered in the shower is gone by the kettle. Capture is where the thought goes before that happens: shopping on one tab, notes on the other, the Journal for the rest. In the same app as your day, free, with no cap. Now you see it.

A long list is a threat. Three is a day.

Dune Seven asks one question each morning: what matters most today? You name up to three. Everything else stays captured, out of the way.

Habits are a guide, not a scorecard, so there is no streak to break and no punishment for the week life got in the way. Time blindness is met with blocks you can see: Today at a glance lays the day out so the gaps become obvious, and the Focus timer shows thirty minutes draining rather than a number to hold in your head.
