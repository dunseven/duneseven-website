Most planners rest on three quiet assumptions: consistent energy, a reliable working memory and an accurate sense of how long things take. ADHD interrupts all three. So the app keeps working perfectly and you keep feeling like the broken part.

Dune Seven starts from the day you actually have. Every task stays reachable in all three modes. What changes is how much you have to face at once, which is usually the real barrier.

When a task becomes a wall.

Tap I’m Stuck and it becomes three to five steps you can actually start. On any task, on any day.

Read on

“Book the appointment” isn’t one task. It’s find the number, check the calendar, decide on a day, make the call, and each of those has its own friction. Task initiation is the wall, not the task.

I’m Stuck never judges the task or the delay. It breaks it down and hands you the first step. Three a month on Free, unlimited on Pro.

Get it out of your head.

A shopping list, notes and a journal, in the same app as your day. If you don’t see it, it isn’t there. Now you see it.

Capture is where the loose ends go so they stop circling. Shopping on one tab, notes on the other, the Journal for the rest. All of it free, with no cap.

Three priorities, not thirty.

A long list is a threat. Dune Seven asks one question each morning: what matters most today? You name up to three. Everything else stays captured, out of the way.

Habits are a guide, not a scorecard. There is no streak to break and no punishment for the week life got in the way. Time blindness is met with blocks you can see, not numbers you have to imagine: Today at a glance lays the day out so the gaps become obvious, and the Focus Countdown Timer shows time draining rather than a figure to hold in your head.
