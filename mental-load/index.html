Mental load

What the mental load actually is

The mental load is the cognitive and organisational labour of running a life: knowing what needs doing, when, for whom, and holding all of it continuously in the background. It isn't the doing. It's the remembering that it needs doing, and the noticing before anyone else has to ask.

It's disproportionately carried by women, it rarely appears on anyone's list of chores, and it doesn't clock off. Most people carrying it don't describe themselves as busy. They describe themselves as never quite able to put it down.

Why more organising makes it worse

The instinctive fix is a better system. More lists, more tags, more colour-coded categories. But every system you build is another thing to maintain, and maintenance is itself mental load. You end up administering the tool instead of being relieved by it.

Dune Seven takes the opposite position. Fewer decisions, less structure to keep up, and a deliberately small daily surface.

Get it out of your head, immediately

Capture exists for the thought that arrives mid-shower, mid-meeting, mid-sentence. Two taps and it's out of your working memory and into somewhere you trust. You sort it later, when sorting is cheap.

Three things, not everything

Each morning Dune Seven asks one question: what matters most today? You name up to three. The rest still exists, still safe, just not shouting. That's your three — everything else can wait.

See where the day actually goes

Today at a glance lays your timed commitments across one calm scrolling strip. Not to optimise you, but so you can finally see the shape of the day and where the open space genuinely is.

A private place to unload

Journal is quiet and entirely private. Sometimes what's weighing isn't a task at all — it's an unprocessed thought taking up the same room. Your Journal, reflections and habits are never shared, on any tier.

Visibility without the mental labour. That's the whole design goal.

When executive dysfunction is part of it

Mental load and executive dysfunction compound each other badly. When working memory is already unreliable, holding a running household index is exhausting in a way that's difficult to explain to anyone who hasn't done it. Dune Seven's energy modes and task breakdown tools were built for exactly that overlap.

Questions

Mental load questions

Isn't this just another app to maintain?

That's the risk with most of them, and it's the thing Dune Seven is designed against. There are no projects to configure, no tags to invent and no folder structures. Capture a thought in two taps, name three priorities a day, and that's the whole system.

Can my partner see what I'm carrying?

A shared-visibility feature is planned so the people you live with can see where you're at without you having to find the energy to explain. Your Journal, reflections and habits are never included, no matter what.

Is it only for mothers?

No. Mental load falls heavily on mothers and on women generally, but it lands on anyone who has become the default rememberer for a household, a team or a family.
