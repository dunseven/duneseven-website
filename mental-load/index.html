You are already running the household in your head.

A planner should reduce what you have to hold, not become one more system to maintain. Fewer decisions, less structure to keep up, a deliberately small daily surface.

Read on

The mental load is the cognitive and organisational labour of running a life: knowing what needs doing, when, for whom, and holding all of it continuously in the background. It is not the doing. It is the remembering that it needs doing, and the noticing before anyone else has to ask.

It is disproportionately carried by women, it rarely appears on anyone’s list of chores, and it does not clock off. The instinctive fix is a better system: more lists, more tags, more colour-coded categories. But every system you build is another thing to maintain, and maintenance is itself mental load. Dune Seven takes the opposite position.

Get it out of your head, immediately.

Capture exists for the thought that arrives mid-shower, mid-meeting, mid-sentence. Two taps and it is out of your working memory and somewhere you trust.

Shopping on one tab, notes on the other, the Journal for what is not a task at all. You sort it later, when sorting is cheap. All of it free, with no cap.

Three things, not everything.

Each morning Dune Seven asks one question: what matters most today? You name up to three. The rest still exists, still safe, just not shouting.

Today at a glance lays your timed commitments across one calm strip, so you can finally see the shape of the day and where the open space really is. Repeating routines hold the load-bearing rhythms once, so you stop re-remembering them. Visibility without the mental labour. That is the whole design goal.

When executive dysfunction is part of it.

Mental load and executive dysfunction compound each other badly. When working memory is already unreliable, holding a running household index is exhausting in a way that is hard to explain.

Dune Seven’s energy modes and I’m Stuck were built for exactly that overlap. On a Low day the home screen quiets down to what you can face. When a task has become a wall, I’m Stuck breaks it into three to five steps you can start.

Mental load questions.

Is not this just another app to maintain?

That is the risk with most of them, and it is the thing Dune Seven is designed against. There are no projects to configure, no tags to invent and no folder structures. Capture a thought in two taps, name three priorities a day, and that is the whole system.

Can my partner see what I am carrying?

A shared-visibility feature is planned so the people you live with can see where you are at without you having to find the energy to explain. Your Journal, reflections and habits are never included, no matter what.

Is it only for mothers?

No. Mental load falls heavily on mothers and on women generally, but it lands on anyone who has become the default rememberer for a household, a team or a family.

What does it cost?
