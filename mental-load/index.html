You’re already running the household in your head.

A planner should reduce what you have to hold, not become one more system to maintain. Fewer decisions, less structure to keep up, a deliberately small daily surface.

Read on

The mental load is the cognitive and organisational labour of running a life: knowing what needs doing, when, for whom, and holding all of it continuously in the background. It isn’t the doing. It’s the remembering that it needs doing, and the noticing before anyone else has to ask.

It’s disproportionately carried by women, it rarely appears on anyone’s list of chores, and it doesn’t clock off. The instinctive fix is a better system: more lists, more tags, more colour-coded categories. But every system you build is another thing to maintain, and maintenance is itself mental load. Dune Seven takes the opposite position.

Get it out of your head, immediately.

Three things, not everything.

When executive dysfunction is part of it.

Mental load and executive dysfunction compound each other badly. When working memory is already unreliable, holding a running household index is exhausting in a way that is hard to explain.

Dune Seven’s energy modes and I’m Stuck were built for exactly that overlap. On a Low day the home screen quiets down to what you can face. When a task has become a wall, I’m Stuck breaks it into three to five steps you can start.

Mental load questions.

Isn’t this just another app to maintain?

That’s the risk with most of them, and it’s the thing Dune Seven is designed against. There are no projects to configure, no tags to invent and no folder structures. Capture a thought in two taps, name three priorities a day, and that’s the whole system.

Can my partner see what I’m carrying?

A shared-visibility feature is planned so the people you live with can see where you’re at without you having to find the energy to explain. Your Journal, reflections and habits are never included, no matter what.

Is it only for mothers?

No. Mental load falls heavily on mothers and on women generally, but it lands on anyone who has become the default rememberer for a household, a team or a family.

What does it cost?

There’s a permanently free tier with no card required. Pro is A$69.99 a year or A$12.99 a month, and Lifetime is a single A$129.99.
