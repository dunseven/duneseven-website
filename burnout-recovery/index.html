Planning through burnout, when the list is the problem

There's a point where the to-do list stops being a tool and becomes the thing you can't face. Dune Seven is built to be usable on exactly those days.

Recovery doesn't run on a productivity system. It runs on a smaller surface, fewer decisions, and permission to have a Low day without it counting against you.

Burnout & overwhelm

What overwhelm does to planning

Burnout narrows capacity in every direction: energy, decision-making, tolerance for stimulus. And a standard planner responds to that narrowing by presenting the same wall of tasks it always did, in the same density, with a streak counter attached.

So opening the app becomes its own aversive event. You avoid the tool, the list grows in your absence, and the gap between where you are and what the app expects widens until you abandon it entirely. That isn't a discipline failure. It's a design mismatch.

Low mode: the app gets smaller when you do

This is the piece nothing else does. Switch to Low and the home screen contracts. Less on screen, fewer decisions, less to face. Nothing is deleted — every task remains reachable in all three modes — but the surface you have to meet shrinks to something survivable.

When capacity returns, Flow opens back up. Focus narrows the world to one thing when that's what's needed. You move between them freely, as many times a day as reality demands.

No streak punishment

Habit tracking in Dune Seven has no cap and no penalty. A missed week is a missed week, not a broken chain and not a red mark. Streaks are shown as a guide, never as a scorecard, because in recovery a scorecard is actively harmful.

Reset my day

Some days need to start over at 2pm. One tap clears the slate, with no summary of everything you didn't manage and no encouragement to try harder.

Three things, maximum

On a burnt-out day, three feels ambitious and thirty feels impossible. Dune Seven caps daily priorities at three by design. Naming one and leaving two blank is a legitimate use of the app.

Plan for the energy you actually have, not the energy you used to have. That distinction is the entire product.

A note on what this is and isn't

Dune Seven is a planner. It isn't therapy, medical treatment or a substitute for either. Burnout that involves persistent low mood, hopelessness or difficulty functioning is worth taking to a GP or a mental health professional — a planner is a support alongside that, not instead of it.

What it can do is stop your organising system from being one more thing making the days harder.

Questions

Burnout and overwhelm questions

I've abandoned every planner I've tried. Why would this be different?

Because the failure point is usually the same: the app expects the same output from you on your worst day as your best. Low mode is a direct answer to that. The app shrinks to match your capacity instead of holding a fixed standard you have to keep meeting.

Will it make me feel guilty for missing days?

No. There's no streak punishment, no red marks, no summary of what you didn't do, and no notifications designed to shame you back into the app.

Is this a mental health app?

No. Dune Seven is a daily planner. It doesn't diagnose, treat or replace professional care. If burnout is affecting your ability to function, please talk to a GP or a mental health professional.

Can I use it just for a few things?

Yes. Naming one priority and leaving the rest empty is a completely valid way to use it. There is no minimum.
