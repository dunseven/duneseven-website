## Section 2 — "What we collect" — replace the Subscriptions bullet

**Currently live:**
> **Subscriptions** — subscriptions and free trials are processed by Apple through the App Store. We receive your purchase history (never your payment or card details) so the app knows what you have access to.

**Replace with:**
> **Subscriptions and Lifetime purchases** — subscriptions, free trials and the one-time Lifetime purchase are processed by Apple through the App Store or Google through Google Play, depending on which device you use. We receive your purchase history (never your payment or card details) so the app knows what you have access to.

**Why this changed:** the old wording only mentioned "subscriptions and free trials," which was accurate when it was written but excludes the Lifetime one-time purchase, now a real product. It also only named Apple, though Google Play carries the same purchase (DUN-88, Play Store submission). This brings the sentence in line with what's actually purchasable today.

## Section 4 — "The 'I'm Stuck' feature and AI" — replace the first two sentences

**Currently live:**
> When you tap "I'm Stuck" on a task, the text of *that task* is sent to **Anthropic**, an AI provider, so it can be broken into smaller steps and sent back to you. That's the only thing that goes — the task text you chose to break down.
>
> Your journal entries, your other tasks, your habits and your energy modes are **not** sent to any AI provider. If you never use "I'm Stuck," nothing from your account is sent to an AI provider at all.

**Replace with:**
> When you use I'm Stuck, your task text is sent to **Anthropic** to break it down. Nothing else you write leaves the app — not your Journal, not your reflections, not your habits.
>
> If you never use "I'm Stuck," nothing from your account is sent to an AI provider at all.

**Why this changed:** this is the exact string locked in the Terminology document, word for word — the same one that governs the AI disclosure on the app's "Your plan" screen. The old page wording said the same thing but in different words, which is the specific kind of drift the Terminology document exists to prevent: one boundary, one statement, everywhere it appears. The second sentence ("If you never use...") is kept as-is from the current page — it doesn't appear in the locked Terminology string, but it's true, useful, and doesn't contradict it, so there's no reason to cut it.
